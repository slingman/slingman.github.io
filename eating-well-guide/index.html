Eating Well Guide

Less rice and noodles, more vegetables and protein. Keep the family favorites, just change the portions.

Build every plate the same way

Use a regular 9-inch dinner plate or bowl.

  • Half: vegetablesBok choy, gai lan, choy sum, napa cabbage, rau muống, bean sprouts, mushrooms, bitter melon, herbs
  • Quarter: proteinFish, shrimp, chicken, tofu, eggs, lean pork
  • Quarter: rice or noodlesAbout ½ to ⅔ cup cooked. Brown or mixed rice is better.

Check with the doctor. This is general guidance. If taking insulin or pills like glipizide or glyburide, skipping meals or cutting carbs a lot can cause low blood sugar.

Dishes to make

Mostly vegetables, protein and broth, with little sugar or starch.

  • Steamed fish with ginger and scallionChineseHigh protein, almost no carbs. Use low-sodium soy sauce.
  • Stir-fried greens with garlicBothBok choy, gai lan, ong choy. Go easy on oyster sauce.
  • Canh chua with fishVietnameseSour soup with tamarind and tomato. Less sugar and pineapple.
  • Canh bí or canh cảiVietnameseWinter melon or mustard green soup. Mostly vegetables and broth.
  • Bitter melon, stuffed with pork or with eggBothBitter melon is very low in carbs.
  • Steamed egg custard (chưng trứng / zing daan)BothProtein with no carbs.
  • Tofu with mushrooms, or mapo tofuChinesePlant protein. Use less oil.
  • Gỏi cuốn with shrimpVietnameseExtra lettuce and herbs, less vermicelli. Make nước chấm with sugar substitute.
  • Gỏi gàVietnameseChicken and cabbage salad with herbs.
  • Thịt kho trứng, modifiedVietnameseUse sugar substitute instead of caramel sugar. Small rice portion.
  • Lettuce wrapsChineseMinced chicken or pork in lettuce instead of over rice.
  • Clear bone soup with daikon and carrotBothFilling and low calorie.
  • Cauliflower fried riceBothOr half cauliflower, half rice. Cuts carbs about in half.
  • Phở at homeVietnameseHalf the noodles, double the sprouts, herbs and meat. Skip hoisin.

Also go easy on

  • Salty sauces: soy, fish sauce, oyster, hoisin. Diabetes raises heart and kidney risk.
  • Fatty meats: pork belly, duck skin, fried foods.
  • Coconut milk curries: fine sometimes, with less rice.

Avoid, or save for rare occasions

These raise blood sugar fast because of sugar, refined starch, or both.

7-day meal plan

Rice or noodles stay around ⅔ cup per meal. Swap days freely; dinner leftovers make good lunches.

Drinks all week: water, unsweetened jasmine, oolong or green tea, black coffee or coffee with a little milk and sweetener.

When to eat

Three meals, 4–5 hours apart, plus a small snack only if needed.

  • 7:30 am · Breakfast
    Within an hour or two of waking. Don't skip it.
  • 12:00 pm · Lunch
  • 3:30 pm · Snack, if hungry
    Protein plus a little carb, or if dinner will be late.
  • 6:30 pm · Dinner
    Finish 2–3 hours before bed.
  • Bedtime
    Small snack only if the doctor recommends it.

Habits that help

  • Keep meal times and rice amounts similar every day.
  • Eat vegetables and protein first, rice or noodles last.
  • Walk 10–15 minutes after meals, especially dinner.
  • Don't snack all day long.
  • Check blood sugar 2 hours after a meal to learn which dishes work.
  • If on insulin or glipizide-type pills, keep glucose tablets or 4 oz juice nearby for lows.
General guidance only. Review portions and carb goals with the doctor or a registered dietitian.
